# Editorial reference notes

References used to review selected claims and pronunciation. They are not a certification of the course or an endorsement by the organisations listed. Content was written for language practice, not copied from these sources as a treatment or emergency manual. Reference review date: 22 September 2026.

| Source | Editorial use |
|---|---|
| [US FDA — Hypoallergenic cosmetics](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling-claims/hypoallergenic-cosmetics) | Avoid teaching “hypoallergenic” as an allergy-free guarantee. The FDA discussion is US-specific; no claim about Thai labelling law is made. |
| [NCCIH — Detoxes and cleanses](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/detoxes-and-cleanses-what-you-need-to-know) | Distinguish wellness marketing from demonstrated toxin-removal effects or medical detoxification. |
| [NCCIH — Massage therapy](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/massage-therapy-what-you-need-to-know) | Avoid universal safety or outcome guarantees; use individual assessment and appropriate referral. |
| [CDC — Preventing norovirus](https://www.cdc.gov/norovirus/prevention/index.html) | Explicit soap-and-water language; sanitiser is not a substitute for handwashing against norovirus. |
| [US FDA — Food allergies](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/food-allergies) | Ingredient checking and escalation instead of an unsupported “safe” promise. |
| [US FDA — Gluten and food labelling](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-education-resources-materials/gluten-and-food-labeling) | Distinguish gluten-related terms; do not infer that every dietary description guarantees suitability. |
| [IMO Resolution MSC.48(66), LSA Code, section 7.2.1.1](https://wwwcdn.imo.org/localresources/en/KnowledgeCentre/IndexofIMOResolutions/MSCResolutions/MSC.48(66).pdf) | Verify the general emergency alarm wording: seven or more short blasts followed by one long. This historical resolution is cited for this wording only, not as a complete current regulatory manual. |
| [Merriam-Webster — Acupressure](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/acupressure) | Primary stress in the initial syllable. |
| [Merriam-Webster — Reflexology](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/reflexology) | Primary stress on the syllable represented as “ol” in the reading guide. |
| [Merriam-Webster Medical — Petrissage](https://www.merriam-webster.com/medical/petrissage) | Final primary stress and corresponding transcription correction. |
| [MDN — SpeechRecognition](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SpeechRecognition) | Browser speech recognition may use a remote service; avoid claiming all audio processing stays on-device. |
| [Council of Europe — CEFR global scale](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-1-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-global-scale) | Support practical familiar-language progression without claiming the app's three labels are CEFR certification. |
| [Institute of Education Sciences — Organizing instruction and study to improve learning](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1) | Background for revisiting learned material and retrieval practice; the chosen app intervals themselves have not been validated. |

## Required local review

A qualified spa practitioner and shipboard training representative should verify operational use. A Thai-language editor should review register and preferred wording. Prices, currencies, actual locations, times, dietary assurances, compensation and emergency arrangements must be confirmed through the relevant workplace's approved procedures. Dictionary transcriptions and browser voices may use different regional pronunciations.


## Spa anatomy vocabulary note (2026-09-25)

The spa course's "Body parts" category was extended from 20 basic external
terms to 53, adding the anatomy a therapist has to name during a treatment:
muscle groups (trapezius, hamstring, calf, glutes), spine and shoulder
landmarks, hand and foot detail, and the facial areas used in a facial.
Definitions are plain-English service descriptions, not clinical anatomy, and
the examples are consultation language rather than treatment instructions.

These entries are editorial: the English, the IPA transcriptions and the Thai
translations have NOT yet had independent review, and the Thai in particular
should be checked by a Thai-language editor alongside the rest of the course
before formal rollout. Level labels are editorial, not CEFR. Terms such as
"muscle knot" and "pressure point" are described as a guest-facing therapist
would describe them, and are not a diagnosis or a clinical claim.

## Quiz/activity expansion note

The new practice tasks were authored for this app. Workplace messages are fictional,
and deliberately incorrect distractors are not endorsed instructions. The current
expansion revisited the NCCIH massage-therapy, CDC norovirus-prevention and Council
of Europe global-scale references listed above. They support cautious wording and
simple practical communication, not certification of every item or current local
workplace procedures. No passage from those sources is used as the reading bank.
